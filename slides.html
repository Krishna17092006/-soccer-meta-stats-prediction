Team 20 Problem Statement #69 English Premier League, 2019–2025

01 · The Problem

Raw match stats are outcomes, not predictors.

Home win, draw, or away win — a three-way call every pundit makes from gut feel. Naive models average a team's own raw numbers: goals, shots, corners. But those numbers are themselves the result of matches already played — they carry weaker forward-looking signal than they seem to.

  • Can we beat a baseline built only on a team's own raw match stats?
  • Does engineering meta-statistics — strength ratings, form, market odds — give a real, measurable edge?

03 · The Core Idea

Two feature sets, same models, head-to-head

Baseline — raw statistics

Rolling averages (last 5 games) of a team's own goals scored, goal difference, and shots.

VS

Meta-statistics

Elo rating, recent form, goal-difference trend, rest days, head-to-head history, and de-vigorized market-implied probability.

Same models trained on each set — so any accuracy gap is caused by the features, not the model.

04 · Methodology

Rule #1: no peeking at the future.

Every feature for a match uses only information available strictly before kickoff. Matches are processed in chronological order; a team's Elo, form, and head-to-head record update only after that match's features are already recorded.

Why it matters

Skip this and the model silently "learns" from information it would never have had before kickoff — inflating accuracy with results that don't hold up on new matches.

08 · What Mattered

The market already knows a lot.

Feature importance — meta-statistics Random Forest.

Market-implied probability and Elo rating difference are the two strongest single predictors — the "wisdom of the crowd" and a compounding strength rating beat any single raw stat.

09 · Conclusions

The statistical edge is real.

  • A majority-class guess ("always Home win") reaches ~41% — the floor to beat.
  • Raw per-match stats, rolling-averaged, barely clear that floor (~50%).
  • Meta-statistics give a consistent ~4-point accuracy lift and lower log-loss, on both a linear and a tree-based model.
  • Combining both sets doesn't beat meta-statistics alone — raw stats add little that Elo, form, and market odds don't already capture.

Limitations: single league, no player-level data. Future work: gradient boosting, multi-league generalization, recency-weighted Elo.
